Venezuela's new cabinet promises calm as released prisoners return home

Seven months into a transition few predicted, officials in Caracas say the worst is over. In the countryside, the picture is less clear.

By a staff correspondent · 6 November 2026 · 6 min read
Illustration. This article is fictional and written for the Ground Truth demo.

Government spokespeople on Tuesday described the country as "stable and recovering", pointing to the release of more than 400 opposition figures since April and a steady rise in oil shipments under new export agreements.

Officials say all released opposition figures are free to travel and speak publicly. Several have already given interviews to local radio stations, and two have announced plans to stand in regional elections expected next year.

The military, long seen as the deciding factor in Venezuelan politics, has shown no visible fractures since the defence minister was replaced. Senior commanders appeared alongside the new cabinet at a ceremony in Caracas last week.

Outside the capital, aid workers describe a slower recovery. Health posts in rural Zulia report patients travelling up to six hours for basic care, and fuel shortages continue to delay deliveries of medicine from regional depots.

Along the Colombian border, crossings have partially reopened. Traders in Táchira say movement is easier than at the start of the year, though checkpoints remain frequent and unpredictable.

The continued presence of US naval vessels in the Caribbean has drawn criticism from some regional governments. Humanitarian groups say port inspections have added days to the arrival of relief supplies, a claim US officials dispute.

Analysts caution that the situation could still change quickly. "The calm in Caracas is real," said one researcher who works in the country, "but it does not tell you much about what is happening in Apure or Zulia."
